For S01, write the proposed character as lambda(a^u b^v)=(-1)^u omega^v. Cubing a kernel equation removes omega and forces u=0 modulo two. The original equation then forces v=0 modulo three. The representation has trivial kernel and degree one. A nontrivial group cannot act faithfully in dimension zero. For general nontrivial invariant factors n1 dividing each later factor, a prime dividing n1 produces a p-torsion subgroup of rank r. Restricting d weights gives a linear map into a rank-d elementary abelian group, so faithfulness forces d at least r. Coordinate weights attain r. This lower-bound proof does not apply to an arbitrary displayed factorization.

For S02, write lambda(a)=i^j and lambda(b)=i^k. Killing every square forces 2j=2k=0 modulo four and is sufficient by multiplicativity. There are four even-exponent pairs. The earlier single-product condition only forces j+k even and has eight solutions. Independently, lambda(a squared b squared) is the product of the two squared-generator values. Those values cannot both be minus one while their product is also minus one. Finite-abelian complex irreducibles are linear, so this covers the required irreducible scope.

For S03, let the finite determinant image have generator zeta and order 2m. It is cyclic because it lies in a finite cyclic root-of-unity group. The homomorphism epsilon=delta to the power m takes only the values one and minus one and attains minus one on a preimage of zeta. The first isomorphism theorem gives a normal kernel of index two. The original determinant kernel instead has index 2m. For an involution with q negative eigenvalues, trace minus degree is minus 2q, so failure of congruence modulo four is equivalent to q odd and determinant minus one. This proves the claimed existence alternative. For the faithful C4 character with generator value i, epsilon=delta squared has kernel consisting of identity and the involution. That involution has trace minus one in degree one and lies inside the new kernel. The stronger exclusion claim would therefore be false.
